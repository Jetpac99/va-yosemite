Florida Keys holidays, one island at a time

Coral reefs, clapboard porches, marina sunsets and a road that seems to skim the sea. The Keys do laid-back beautifully, but never lazily.

The Florida Keys are not one destination so much as a string of very different moods, tied together by US 1 and a lot of glittering water. Start in Key Largo for reefs and mangroves, drift through Islamorada and Marathon for boating, seafood and family-friendly stops, then keep going until Key West brings the sunset applause.

Flight schedule

Virgin Atlantic flies daily to Miami from London Heathrow, putting Key Largo around 55 miles away and Key West around 160 miles away by road.

Road-trip rhythm

The Overseas Highway is a 113-mile drive with ocean views, 42 bridges and mile markers that count down to zero in Key West.

Reef etiquette

The Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary protects coral reefs, seagrass, mangroves and thousands of marine species. Look, linger, but do not touch.

Worth planning ahead

Dry Tortugas, the day trip with bragging rights

Almost 70 miles west of Key West, Dry Tortugas National Park is mostly open water, seven small islands and one magnificent brick fort. Go for the ferry or seaplane arrival, stay for snorkelling around history.

Florida Keys holiday highlights

Choose your Keys pace

John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park was the country's first undersea park, and the wider sanctuary is home to coral reefs, seagrass meadows and mangrove-fringed islands. Book responsible operators, use reef-safe habits and give the underwater world its space.

Make the drive part of the holiday. Stop for Key Largo water time, Islamorada marina lunches, Marathon's Seven Mile Bridge views and the Lower Keys before rolling into Key West for golden hour.

Order the fish sandwich, say yes to Key lime pie and leave room for sunset. The best meals here often come with wooden decks, pelicans nearby and a bill that smells faintly of sunscreen.

A 7 day Florida Keys itinerary

Days 1-2

Miami to Key Largo

Land in Miami, pick up your car and ease into the Keys with reef trips, mangrove kayaking and an early night by the water.

Days 3-4

Islamorada and Marathon

Build in boat time, seafood stops, turtle conservation, Seven Mile Bridge views and one slow morning with no useful plan.

Days 5-7

Lower Keys and Key West

Watch the landscape loosen, then finish with Old Town, Mallory Square sunsets and, if you are organised, Dry Tortugas.

When to visit the Florida Keys

Winter and spring are favourites for warm days and lower humidity. Summer brings serious heat, warm seas and a livelier storm season, so keep plans flexible and check official advice before you travel.

Florida Keys holiday FAQs

How many days do you need in the Florida Keys?

Seven days gives you a satisfying first trip, especially if you split time between Key Largo or Islamorada, Marathon and Key West. Add more time if you want Dry Tortugas or a slower Miami start.

Do you need a car in the Florida Keys?

Yes for most holidays. A car gives you the freedom to follow the Overseas Highway, stop at beaches and restaurants, and move between different Keys without planning around transfers.

Where should first-time visitors stay?

Key Largo is easy after Miami and great for reefs. Islamorada feels polished but relaxed. Marathon is practical for families and road trips. Key West is the classic finish if you want nightlife, history and sunset theatre.

Can you visit Dry Tortugas from Key West?

Yes, but plan ahead. Dry Tortugas National Park is accessible only by boat or seaplane, and popular departures can book up early.
